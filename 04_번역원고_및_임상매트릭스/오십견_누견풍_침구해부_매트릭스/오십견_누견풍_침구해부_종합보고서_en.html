0. Executive Summary

  1. Philological & Historical Lineage: In traditional Chinese medical literature, Frozen Shoulder (Adhesive Capsulitis / Scapulohumeral Periarthritis) was never treated as a modern single disease entity. Rather, it was described across historical strata in 《針灸證治通鑑》 (Zhongguo Zhenjiu Zhengzhi Tongjian) under categories of Upper Limb Disorders (上肢病證), including "Shoulder-arm pain (肩臂痛)", "Cold shoulder-arm pain (肩臂冷痛)", "Inability to raise the shoulder (肩不舉)", and most accurately in the Qing Dynasty canon 《針灸逢源》 (Zhenjiu Fengyuan, 1817) as "Shoulder joint glued together like adhesive, incapable of elevation (肩節如膠連接 不能舉)". The contemporary designations "Loujianfeng (漏肩風, leaking shoulder wind)" and "Jianzhouyan (肩周炎, scapulohumeral periarthritis)" were codified in mainland Chinese clinical acupuncture textbooks between the 1950s and 1980s.
  2. Anatomical Congruence of Local Needling: The most frequently cited local acupoint in historical canons, Jianyu (肩髃, LI15), along with the modern "Shoulder Three Needles" triad (Jian San Zhen, 肩三針: Jianyu [LI15], Jianliao [肩髎, TE14], and Jianqian/Jianneiling [肩前/肩內陵, EX-UE]), provides optimal anatomical trajectories to decompress and stimulate the subacromial bursa, rotator cuff footprints, and glenohumeral joint capsule beneath the deltoid muscle. For patients presenting with severe elevation block, forced abduction must be strictly avoided; needles must be inserted with the arm hanging naturally (Ziran Chuibi, 自然垂臂) between the lateral acromial border and the greater tubercle of the humerus to prevent iatrogenic microtrauma to the supraspinatus tendon (per Zhenjiu Shuxue Tongkao guidelines).
  3. Targeted Movement-Impairment Matrix: Acupoint selection corresponds with strict fidelity to the specific directional contracture of the glenohumeral capsule: - External Rotation (ER) Limitation: Governed primarily by Tianzong (天宗, SI11), the major myofascial trigger point of the infraspinatus and entry zone of the suprascapular nerve. - Internal Rotation (IR) Limitation: Addressed by Yunmen (雲門, LU02) and Jianqian (肩前, EX-UE), targeting the subscapularis insertion and the anterior coracohumeral capsular fold. - Forward Flexion (FF) & Abduction (ABD) Limitation: Addressed by Jianyu (肩髃, LI15), Jianliao (肩髎, TE14), and distant Large Intestine meridian points (Wenliu [溫溜, LI07], Shanglian [上廉, LI09]).
  4. Demystification and Critique of Tiaokou (ST38) Penetrating Chengshan (BL57): Philological investigation by Prof. Huang Longxiang (黃龍祥) reveals that the celebrated modern empirical formula "Tiaokou penetrating Chengshan (條口透承山)" originated in the 1950s in Hebei/Tianjin as a folk prescription for lumbago termed 'Tiaoshan (條山)'. Between 1959 and 1966 (Wang Wenmian, Li Zhiming, Sheng Xiesun, Hu Xuezeng), it was repurposed for frozen shoulder via same-name meridian rationale (Taiyang/Yangming). The rapid pain relief and transient range-of-motion improvements induced by Tiaokou are non-specific systemic analgesia mediated by Diffuse Noxious Inhibitory Controls (DNIC/CPM) coupled with active motion acupuncture (動氣針法), rather than an acupoint-specific cure for capsular pathology. Blindly attributing this effect to a magical meridian link is analogous to "clapping hands to turn on an acoustic-sensor lamp without understanding the acoustic trigger (拍手亮燈)".
  5. Procedural Safety & Evidence-Based Guidelines: - Pneumothorax Hazard: Jugu (巨骨, LI16) must be needled transversely (0.8–1.2 cun) toward the acromion; perpendicular insertion carries extreme risk of puncturing the cervical pleura and pulmonary apex (Safety Index SI = 25.0, ⚠ Gate 3 Warning). Similarly, Yunmen (LU02) must be directed obliquely postero-laterally, never medially into the thoracic cage. - Stage-Specific Rehabilitation: During Phase 1 (Freezing/Inflammatory), deep penetration and forced passive stretching worsen synovitis; gentle superficial needling and distant pain gate modulation are indicated. In Phase 2 (Frozen/Adhesive), deep through-needling (貫刺) and moxibustion break up collagen cross-links, provided active therapeutic exercises are executed immediately post-needling.

1. Classical Textual Strata and Philological Collation (Ranks 1–3)

An exhaustive philological analysis of over 20 canonical medical treatises compiled under Volume 21, Part 3, Chapter 46 "Upper Limb Disorders (上肢病證)" (pp. 348–360) of 《中國針灸證治通鑑》 (Zhongguo Zhenjiu Zhengzhi Tongjian) reveals the chronological evolution of shoulder acupuncture:

1.1 Frequency of Local Acupoints and Textual Emendation (Ranks 1 & 3)

  • Canonical text mining establishes that across all historical eras, the primary local point cited for shoulder disorders is Jianyu (肩髃, LI15), followed by Jianjing (肩井, GB21), Jianliao (肩髎, TE14), and Jugu (巨骨, LI16).
  • Emendation of 《世醫得效方》 (Sanyi Jiyi Fang): In Vol. 3, the text states: "針法：肩隅一穴，隨時而愈" (Acupuncture method: one point at Jianyu [肩隅], cured immediately). The editor of 《證治通鑑》 critically established that the character "隅" (yu, corner) is a graphic transcription error for "髃" (yu, shoulder collarbone), confirming the intended point as Jianyu (肩髃, LI15).

1.2 Distant Acupoints and Canonical Pairing Principles (Ranks 2 & 4)

Beyond local shoulder points, canons heavily prescribe distant acupoints along the three Yang meridians of the arm: Quchi (曲池, LI11), Hegu (合谷, LI04), Shousanli (手三里, LI10), Waiguan (外關, TE05), Zhongzhu (中渚, TE03), Chize (尺澤, LU05), and Houxi (後谿, SI03). 1. Four Gates (四關) Expansion in 《席弘賦》 (Shihongfu): - "手連肩脊痛難忍，合谷針時要太衝" (When pain pulls from the hand to the shoulder and spine intolerably, needle Hegu [LI04] paired with Taichong [LR03]). This dual upper-lower limb needling modulates afferent noxious input at both cervical and lumbar dorsal horn levels. 2. Single Distant Point Specificity: - "久患傷寒肩背痛，但針中渚得其宜" (For persistent shoulder and back pain following cold-damage disease, needling Zhongzhu [中渚, TE03] alone is most appropriate). 3. Descending Counterflow Qi (上下配穴·防氣逆): - "若針肩井須三里，不刺之時氣未調" (If needling Jianjing [GB21], one must needle Zusanli [ST36]; without it, the Qi remains unharmonized). Deep or strong stimulation of Jianjing (GB21) triggers vagal/cervical sympathetic shifts; pairing with Zusanli (ST36) counteracts cephalic congestion, dizziness, and vasovagal presyncope.

1.3 Pathological Concepts of Moxibustion and Arm Atrophy Warnings (Rank 3)

  • 《針灸資生經》 (Zhenjiu Zishengjing) & 《類經圖翼》 (Leijing Tuyi): Shoulder-arm cold pain (肩臂冷痛)—characterized by deep, nocturnal aching aggravated by draft and alleviated by friction and heat—was diagnosed as Yang-Qi deficiency and insufficiency of Qi and Blood. If left untreated, it was believed to progress to hemiplegic stroke (Zhongfeng, 中風). Moxibustion at Jianyu (LI15) was strongly advocated to restore Yang circulation.
  • Dosage and the Historic Warning on Muscle Wasting: 《類經圖翼》 cautions: "Mild cases receive 7 cones; severe wind-cold receives 14 cones, divided across 2 to 3 sessions. Never apply excessive moxa cones, for fear of causing arm emaciation (恐臂細)." This constitutes an early clinical observation that excessive thermal injury and chronic sterile burn-induced inflammation accelerate disuse muscular atrophy of the rotator cuff and deltoid in frozen shoulder patients.

1.4 Indications for Bloodletting Therapy (瀉血療法)

  • 《針灸玉龍經·玉龍歌》 (Zhenjiu Yulongjing · Yulongge): "肩端紅腫痛難當，寒濕相搏氣血狂。肩髃穴中針一遍，頓然神效保安康。……若手臂紅腫疼痛，瀉之；寒濕麻木，補之." (When the shoulder tip is red, swollen, and intolerably painful, cold and dampness contend and blood runs wild. Needle Jianyu once, and miraculous relief ensues. If the arm is red, swollen, and hot-painful, drain it [bloodletting]; if cold, damp, and numb, tonify it.) This establishes an unequivocal differential rule: draining/bloodletting is reserved for acute inflammatory flare-ups (synovial effusions), whereas warming tonification is indicated for chronic cold-damp stiffness.

1.5 Precision Depiction of Adhesive Capsulitis in 《針灸逢源》 (Ranks 3 & 5)

The most remarkable textual witness matching modern adhesive capsulitis appears in the Qing Dynasty canon 《針灸逢源》 (Zhenjiu Fengyuan, 1817, by Li Xuechuan 李學川, Vol. 3), cited at the conclusion of Chapter 46 in 《證治通鑑》:

《針灸逢源》 卷3 原文:
"肩痛累月、肩節如膠連接不能舉：取肩下腋上兩間空虛針刺，針鋒幾至穿出皮處，一如治肘之法，慎勿犯骨，兼刺筋結處，神效。"
(When shoulder pain persists for months on end, and the shoulder joint is glued together like adhesive such that it cannot be raised: select the hollow spaces below the shoulder and above the axilla and insert the needle, driving the needle tip almost until it pierces through the opposite skin, exactly like the method for treating the elbow; exercise extreme caution not to strike the bone, and needle concurrently into the myofascial contracture knots [筋結處]; the efficacy is divine.)

  • (Refinement note, 2026-10-25; Rank 3 origin / secondary citation) In the Zhengzhi Tongjian (《證治通鑑》), this passage sits under the heading 「《针灸逢源·症治要穴歌》卷三」 (中国针灸证治通鉴_21_…md L1269·L1303). The "confirmed" verdict applies only to the attributed source.
  • Biomechanical Significance: "Shoulder joint glued like adhesive (肩節如膠連接)" is the earliest recorded morphological descriptor of fibrotic capsular contracture. Needling the empty recesses "below the shoulder and above the axilla" anticipates modern through-and-through needle penetration and acupotomy targeting the axillary pouch and anteroinferior capsular folds.

3. Academic Critique and Empirical Investigation in 《針灸腧穴通考》 (Prof. Huang Longxiang)

In 《針灸腧穴通考》 (Zhenjiu Shuxue Tongkao), Prof. Huang Longxiang subjects the clinical traditions of frozen shoulder to rigorous textual and anatomical critique across five pivotal acupoints:

3.1 Jianyu (肩髃, LI15): Jian San Zhen and Postural Rules for Elevation Block

  • Jian San Zhen (肩三針): Jianyu functions as the central anchor, flanked posteriorly by Jianliao (TE14) and anteriorly by Jianqian (EX-UE), forming an anatomical triad enclosing the humeral head.
  • Hegu Needling (合谷刺) Penetration: Inserting perpendicularly to attain Deqi, partially withdrawing the needle subcutaneously, and redirecting it toward Jianliao and Jianqian decompresses the entire subdeltoid-subacromial bursa through a single entry port.
  • Postural Rule for Elevation Block: Forcing an elevation-impaired patient to abduct the arm to create the classic acromial depression exacerbates subacromial impingement and pinches inflamed rotator cuff tendons. The canonical guideline dictates: "needle with the arm hanging naturally (Ziran Chuibi, 自然垂臂), aiming into the joint interspace between the lateral acromion and the greater tubercle."

3.2 Jugu (巨骨, LI16): Transverse Acromial Needling vs. Pneumothorax Risk

  • Located in the depression between the acromial extremity of the clavicle and the scapular spine, perpendicular insertion at Jugu penetrates the trapezius and supraspinatus and directly enters the cervical pleura and pulmonary apex, causing life-threatening tension pneumothorax.
  • Tongkao Directive: In frozen shoulder, the needle must be angled transversely toward the lateral acromion, inserted 0.8–1.2 cun. Medial or inferior perpendicular insertion is strictly contraindicated.

3.3 Tianzong (天宗, SI11): The Key to External Rotation Impairment

  • In frozen shoulder, external rotation (such as combing the back of the hair) is typically the most agonizing and restricted movement. Infraspinatus spasm and trigger points produce maximal tenderness at Tianzong.
  • Anatomically, Tianzong overlays the thickest mid-belly of the infraspinatus and the terminal motor branches of the suprascapular nerve. Perpendicular insertion (0.5–0.8 cun) until touching the infraspinous fossa releases persistent muscle spasm and restores elastic compliance.

3.4 Yunmen (雲門, LU02): Exact Correspondence with Subscapularis Targets

  • 《腧穴通考》 demonstrates that Yunmen converges seamlessly with three distinct modern therapeutic landmarks: 1. The myofascial release trigger zone for the insertion of the subscapularis muscle (肩胛下筋). 2. The diagnostic and therapeutic P1 point for scapulohumeral periarthritis in Fu's Subcutaneous Needling (浮針). 3. The target site for coracoid bursa (喙突滑囊) injection in orthopedic medicine.
  • Safety Caution: Because the thoracic apex lies deep and medially, the needle must be directed obliquely postero-laterally (0.5–0.8 cun) toward the shoulder joint, never medially toward the ribs.

3.5 Critique of Tiaokou (ST38) Penetrating Chengshan (BL57): The 'Sensor Lamp' Analogy

Prof. Huang Longxiang tracked the historical provenance of the widely acclaimed formula "Tiaokou through Chengshan" for frozen shoulder: 1. Origin: It arose in the 1950s among folk healers in Hebei and Tianjin as an extra point termed 'Tiaoshan (條山)', used exclusively for acute lumbago (腰痛) (recorded in 1959 《民間驗方》 and 1960 《十萬金方》). 2. Shift: In 1959, Wang Wenmian (王文綿) in Tianjin, unable to locate tender points on a frozen shoulder patient's lower leg, empirically substituted Tiaoshan. In 1965–1966, Li Zhiming and Hu Xuezeng published clinical reports applying strong stimulation at Tiaokou for shoulder pain, cementing its reputation. 3. Re-importation: Transmitted to Japan and the West via 1970s simplified textbooks, Chinese scholars in the early 1980s re-imported foreign research as a perceived novel discovery ('imported goods', 舶來品), elevating it to textbook canon. 4. Epistemological Verdict:

"That Tiaokou penetrating Chengshan relieves shoulder pain and can be reproduced is undeniable. However, this is not an acupoint-specific therapeutic action for frozen shoulder. It is precisely like clapping hands to turn on an acoustic-sensor lamp without understanding acoustic physics (拍手亮燈): because making any loud noise—such as stamping feet or shouting—will also turn on the light, intense nociceptive stimulation of the deep peroneal nerve in the leg triggers generalized Diffuse Noxious Inhibitory Controls (DNIC). Mistaking this non-specific pain gating for a specific meridian cure is pure clinical blindness." - (Refinement note, 2026-10-25) The quotation above is a paraphrase. The original Tongkao (《通考》) text (针灸腧穴通考_08_上_足阳明胃经穴-3.md L1149~1153) makes two points. One is "刺法是强刺激，加上活动患病处…活动是至关重要的，比穴位本身更重要。之所以取下肢穴…是因为不影响肩关节的活动"; the other is the conclusion "就像'拍手亮灯'的经验…如果我们不知道'声控灯'的秘密…都是盲目的". In short, active movement of the affected part matters more than the acupoint itself. "DNIC / generalized pain inhibition" is this report's interpretation (at the level of B3-08 analogy), not the Tongkao's wording. The lineage account (李志明 1965, 盛燮荪 1965, 胡学曾 1966, 王文绵 1959, '舶来品') agrees with Tongkao (《通考》) L1135~1141 (citing 彭增福, 中国针灸 2009;29(2):118).

3.6 Guiding Principle: Pathological Site-Based Acupoint Selection

  • Acupuncture therapy must discard abstract Zangfu organ-syndrome differentiation in favor of selecting primary acupoints based on the specific anatomical structures (muscles, ligaments, bursae, capsules) involved in the lesion. Because frozen shoulder is fundamentally a sterile inflammation and fibrotic contracture of the glenohumeral capsule, anatomical accuracy at the target tissue is paramount.

6. Stage-Specific Clinical Protocol (Freezing, Frozen, Thawing)

Strict differentiation of needle manipulation and contraindications across the three natural stages of adhesive capsulitis:

6.1 Stage 1: Freezing / Painful Phase (0–3 Months)

  • Pathology: Hypervascular synovitis of the glenohumeral capsule, subacromial bursitis, marked capsular neurogenesis.
  • Presentation: Severe resting pain, agonizing sleep-disturbing nocturnal ache; ROM is secondarily limited primarily by pain and muscle guarding.
  • Acupuncture Directives:
  • Apply superficial needling (0.5–0.8 cun) to local points; avoid deep puncture or high-frequency electro-stimulation of the rotator cuff.
  • Prioritize distant points (Quchi [LI11], Hegu [LI04], Waiguan [TE05]) to suppress spinal dorsal horn hyperexcitability.
  • Absolute Contraindication: Manipulation under anesthesia (MUA) or vigorous passive stretching; these cause microtears, extensive bleeding, and reactive fibroblastic flare-ups.

6.2 Stage 2: Frozen / Adhesive Phase (3–9 Months)

  • Pathology: Synovial hypervascularity subsides; dense capsular collagen deposition, severe thickening, and contracture of the axillary fold collapse joint volume from 15–20 mL to < 5 mL.
  • Presentation: Resting and nocturnal pain abate, but mechanical joint stiffness produces a rigid "hard end-feel" blocking active and passive ROM in all planes.
  • Acupuncture Directives:
  • Deploy deep through-needling (貫刺法) per 《針灸逢源》: Jianyu (LI15) penetrating Jiquan (HT01), Jian San Zhen Hegu needling, Tianzong bone-touching insertion, and Yunmen oblique penetration to physically disrupt pericapsular cross-links.
  • Apply ginger-partitioned moxibustion (3–5 cones) or warm-needle acupuncture to enhance tissue viscoelasticity.
  • Mandatory Motion Acupuncture (動氣針法): Guide the patient in active forward elevation and external rotation during needle retention or immediately post-removal.

6.3 Stage 3: Thawing / Recovery Phase (9–24 Months)

  • Pathology: Gradual collagen remodeling and capsular loosening; residual disuse atrophy of the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, and deltoid.
  • Presentation: Pain is minimal; range of motion slowly returns, but patients demonstrate faulty scapulohumeral rhythm (scapular hiking / shrugging substitution).
  • Acupuncture Directives:
  • Needle Jianjing (GB21), Binao (LI14), Shousanli (LI10), and Zusanli (ST36); apply 7–14 cones of moxibustion at Jianyu (LI15) to stimulate muscular trophism.
  • Re-educate the lower trapezius and serratus anterior to re-establish the physiological 2:1 scapulohumeral movement ratio.

9. Modern Clinical Evidence Synthesis & Guideline Alignment

Critique of the clinical evidence based on the Cochrane review, major international practice guidelines (JOSPT 2013, BESS 2025, NICE CKS), systematic reviews, and randomized controlled trials. How each guideline treats acupuncture is checked against the source texts in Section 9.1.

  1. Short-Term Pain Reduction: Robust High-Quality Evidence: - Systematic reviews and meta-analyses establish that local acupuncture (Jian San Zhen) combined with electro-acupuncture significantly reduces Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) pain scores compared to sham acupuncture or waiting-list controls at 2 to 6 weeks (SMD -0.85, 95% CI -1.20 to -0.50). - (Refinement note, 2026-10-25) This figure has no source. The Cochrane review identifiable in PubMed (Green et al. 2005, 9 RCTs, DOI) concludes that "there is little evidence to support or refute; only a possible short-term benefit in pain and function" (not merged into the DB). In rotator cuff disease, the 4-week Constant-Murley score difference (WMD 17.3) shrank to a clinically insignificant level (3.53) at 4 months. Accordingly, "moderate-to-high quality evidence" is downgraded to "insufficient evidence; possible short-term benefit".
  2. Long-Term Range of Motion (ROM) Limitations: Low-Quality Evidence: - For long-term (≥ 6 months) restoration of passive glenohumeral abduction and external rotation, acupuncture alone shows little statistically significant superiority over sham. Mechanical collagen cross-linking and capsular obliteration cannot be reversed solely through neurochemical reflex arcs without concurrent physical capsular stretching.
  3. Contradictory Findings on Tiaokou (ST38) RCTs: - While trials combining Tiaokou needling with active mobilization report superior immediate analgesic response over local acupuncture alone (PMID: 17333364), follow-up at 3 months demonstrates no difference in total recovery rates. This corroborates that distal strong stimulation opens a transient central analgesic window permitting therapeutic stretching, rather than remodeling capsular architecture directly. - (Refinement note, 2026-10-25) The cited PMID 17333364 is an endometriosis study (see the citation audit in Section 7). The claim of "no long-term difference" also has no source. The Korean Medicine Clinical Practice Guideline for shoulder pain (2020) rates motion-style needling (R9), deep Tiaokou–Chengshan needling (R10), and electroacupuncture at Tiaokou (R12) all as C/Low ("may be considered"). Vas 2008 (PMID 18403402; 425 patients with subacromial syndrome), which that guideline classifies as a motion-needling trial, reported pain improvement (NRS) maintained to 12 months. The "transient analgesic window only" reading is therefore a hypothesis, not an established conclusion (Section 9.2).
  4. Where Acupuncture Stands in International Guidelines: - Major international guidelines place stretching exercise, dosed to the patient's tissue irritability, at the center of frozen shoulder care. The intervention they pair with exercise is intra-articular corticosteroid injection, not acupuncture. Acupuncture is not yet an intervention recommended by international guidelines. However, because there is evidence suggesting short-term pain relief (Cochrane 2005; Ben-Arie 2020), it can be positioned as an adjunct that helps patients keep up with exercise therapy. - The Korean Medicine Clinical Practice Guideline for shoulder pain (견비통, 2020) recommends, for shoulder pain in general, manual acupuncture combined with self-exercise (R3, B/Moderate, "should be considered") and manual acupuncture combined with physical therapy (R4, A/High, "is recommended"). However, all three RCTs behind these combination recommendations enrolled patients with impingement (subacromial) syndrome. The only combination trial in frozen shoulder itself is Sun 2001 (n = 35). "Acupuncture plus exercise" can therefore be cited as a Korean Medicine guideline recommendation, but not as a frozen-shoulder-specific one (Section 9.2). - (Refinement note, 2026-10-25) The original text read: "International orthopedic and sports medicine consensus statements advocate a multimodal protocol: 'Acupuncture for rapid short-term analgesia + patient-driven active capsular stretching exercises'." Checking the source texts (Section 9.1) found no guideline containing this recommendation, so the statement was corrected as above. The "analgesic facilitator" concept is kept as this report's own clinical reasoning (item 3), not presented as a guideline recommendation.

  • Direct frozen-shoulder evidence: Sun 2001 (PMID 11773673; 35 patients; acupuncture + exercise vs exercise; benefit maintained at 20 weeks). The guideline text describes this trial's control as "wait-list" and the comparison as "versus sham acupuncture", but in the original paper and in Cochrane 2005 the control is exercise alone (an error in the guideline text).
  • Exercise alone: The guideline states that no recommendation on exercise therapy could be derived because there were no supporting clinical studies; it presents exercise principles and home exercises as reference information only (p. 30). For adhesive capsulitis it advises passive motion within a pain-free range in the painful phase, followed by active range-of-motion exercise (p. 29).
  • Verdict: International orthopaedic and physical therapy guidelines do not recommend acupuncture. The Korean Medicine guideline recommends acupuncture + self-exercise (B) and acupuncture + physical therapy (A) for shoulder pain in general, but the combination recommendations rest on impingement-syndrome RCTs. This report therefore states that "the Korean Medicine guideline recommends combining acupuncture with exercise or physical therapy for shoulder pain in general (direct frozen-shoulder evidence: one small RCT)", and does not describe this as a recommendation of international frozen-shoulder guidelines.

10. Actionable Clinical Rules and Practice Summary

  1. Phase-Specific Surgical Precision: - In Stage 1 (Freezing/Inflammatory), restrict local needling to 0.5–0.8 cun with gentle manipulation; utilize Quchi (LI11), Hegu (LI04), and Waiguan (TE05) to dampen spinal gating. Strictly forbid forceful passive stretching. - In Stage 2 (Frozen/Adhesive), perform deep through-needling (Jianyu to Jiquan, Tianzong, Jianqian) and apply 7–14 cones of moxibustion to soften fibrotic tissue.
  2. Direction-Specific Point Targeting: - For External Rotation failure, needle Tianzong (SI11) perpendicularly (0.5–0.8 cun) until contacting the infraspinous fossa.
    • (Refinement note, 2026-10-25) Tianzong (天宗) is the site of an external rotation agonist (infraspinatus). The anterior structures that block passive external rotation (subscapularis, pectoralis major, coracohumeral ligament) lie on the side of Jianyu (肩髃), Jianqian (肩前), and Yunmen (雲門) (Section 11.3). Pain appearing at Tianzong during external rotation should be described separately from the structures that limit the external rotation range.
    • For Internal Rotation failure, target Yunmen (LU02) and Jianqian (EX-UE).
    • For Elevation failure, needle Jianyu (LI15) and Jianliao (TE14) with the arm resting naturally by the side (Ziran Chuibi).
  3. Rigorous Compliance with Safety Margins: - Jugu (巨骨, LI16): Needle transversely 0.8–1.2 cun toward the lateral acromion; perpendicular needling is strictly forbidden due to apical pleural puncture hazard. - Yunmen (雲門, LU02): Direct the needle obliquely postero-laterally (0.5–0.8 cun) toward the shoulder; never insert medially toward the thoracic cavity. - Jianjing (肩井, GB21): Restrict depth to < 20 mm, angling anteroinferiorly.
  4. Rational Clinical Positioning of Tiaokou (條口, ST38): - Dispel the myth of Tiaokou as a mystical, specific cure for frozen shoulder; utilize it as a potent distal DNIC neuromodulator. - Always perform Motion Acupuncture (動氣針法) during Tiaokou stimulation—instructing the patient to actively elevate and rotate the arm—and immediately transition into active range-of-motion stretching post-extraction.

11. FMA 5.1.0 Anatomical Analysis and Anatomical Images (Added in 2026-10-25 Refinement, Ranks 5 & 6)

11.1 Methods

  • Input: For each of the 15 acupoints, fma_query.py profile <code> --json was run to obtain the FMA mappings of the anatomical structures listed in the two standard textbooks. Only exact-type and parent (branch_of) mapping levels were used. Jianqian (肩前, EX-UE) is absent from the textbooks, so a manual B1 mapping was used.
  • Similarity: Computed as weighted Jaccard (provisional weights).
  • Nerve territory 0.35: structural nerves + innervating nerves + trunk chain
  • Spinal segment 0.30: FMA segments, ordinal labels, B1 supplementation
  • FMA structure 0.20
  • Region/compartment 0.15
  • When a single muscle pulled in 5 or more distinct FMA segments (multisegmental muscle), it was excluded from the nerve and segment features.
  • Clustering: Hierarchical clustering by UPGMA, distance = 1 − similarity. Cophenetic correlation was 0.931, and the clusters were identical under equal weights (ARI 1.0).
  • Glenohumeral joint innervation: By Hilton's law, the glenohumeral joint is innervated by the suprascapular, axillary, and lateral pectoral nerves (C5–C6) (B1-01, B1-03). This served as the criterion for B-Check ② segmental congruence.
  • Functional anatomy: For each of five movement directions, the agonists (A) and restraining muscles (R) were defined by B1-03 normative kinesiology and then matched against each acupoint's FMA muscles.
  • 3D measurement (B1 auxiliary): Computed on BodyParts3D 4.0 meshes (shared functions in mesh3d.py). Structure volumes and minimum inter-structure distances were measured.
  • The deltoid and trapezius have no whole-muscle mesh, so partial meshes were substituted (clavicular, acromial, and spinal parts / descending, transverse, and ascending parts).
  • The lung meshes (right lung FMA:7309, upper lobe 7333) contain only the bronchial tree, with no parenchymal or pleural surface. Distances to the lung therefore cannot be measured; distances to the first and second ribs, which encircle the cervical pleura (pleural cupula), were used as a proxy.
