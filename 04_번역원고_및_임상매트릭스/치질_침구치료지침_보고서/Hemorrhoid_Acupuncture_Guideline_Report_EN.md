# Clinical Practice Guideline for Acupuncture Treatment of Hemorrhoids
### Neuroanatomical Mechanisms and Textual-Critical Clinical Protocols

**Department of Meridian & Acupoint, Wonkwang University College of Korean Medicine · 2026-11-01**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Hemorrhoids represent a pathological displacement and enlargement of the normal submucosal 'anal cushions', accompanied by engorgement of the **anorectal venous plexus (FMA:18933)**, disruption of supporting connective tissue (Parks ligament and Treitz muscle), and subsequent prolapse, thrombosis, or bleeding.

This monograph applies the **Acupoint Anatomy Logic Framework (v2.7)**, the Foundational Model of Anatomy (FMA v5.1.0), and canonical classics (notably *Zhongguo Zhenjiu Zhengzhi Tongjian* and *Zhenjiu Shuxue Tongkao*) to formulate an evidence-based clinical guideline for acupuncture and moxibustion treatment of hemorrhoids. Furthermore, it addresses the widespread clinical use of **Kongzui (LU6)**, clarifying that its hemorrhoidal indication is not derived from classical East Asian medicine literature, but originated from the 20th-century Japanese clinician **Ken Sawada (澤田健)**.

- **Core Classical Architecture**: The orthodox classical formula consists of an integrated triad: **Local-Sacral Acupoints (Changqiang GV1, Ciliao BL32)**, **Distal Lower Limb Acupoints (Chengshan BL57)**, and **Systemic Ascent Acupoints (Baihui GV20)**.
- **Historiographical Refinement on Kongzui (LU6)**:
  - Canonical literature prior to the 20th century (*Huangdi Neijing*, *Zhenjiu Jiayijing*, *Qianjin Yaofang*, *Zhenjiu Dacheng*, etc.) contains **zero records** associating LU6 with hemorrhoids or rectal bleeding.
  - LU6 was first introduced as a specific hemorrhoid point in the 1930s by Japanese acupuncturist **Ken Sawada**, recorded in Bunshi Shirota's *Shinkyu Shinzui* (1941) as Sawada's "original empirical point" located 3 finger-breadths below Chize (LU5).
  - Modern explanations attributing its efficacy to being the "Xi-cleft point of the Lung Meridian controlling acute hemorrhage" or "Lung-Large Intestine interior-exterior relation" are ex-post-facto rationalizations devised when Sawada's empirical point was integrated into 1950–60s modern textbooks (Cho & Kim, 2021). Thus, classical orthodox acupoints and modern empirical points must be strictly distinguished.
- **Biomedical Mechanisms**:
  1. **Local Myofascial & Hemodynamic Regulation**: GV1 targets the inferior rectal nerve (FMA:21865, from pudendal nerve S2-S4) and the levator ani muscle (FMA:19087), mitigating anal sphincter spasms and decompressing the rectal venous plexus.
  2. **Somato-visceral Reflex & S2 Convergence**: BL57 (tibial nerve, L5-S2) converges at the S2 spinal cord segment with visceral and somatic afferents from the anorectum, reflexively modulating pelvic floor resting pressure and accelerating venous drainage.
  3. **Pelvic Floor Suspension**: Moxibustion at GV20 recruits brainstem-spinal motor tracks to restore structural baseline tone in the levator ani, reducing prolapsed hemorrhoidal masses.
- **Stage-Specific Protocols**:
  - **Acute Inflammatory & Pain Stage**: GV1 + BL57 + BL32 (reducing method) + GV28 (pricking/collateral bloodletting).
  - **Acute Bleeding Stage**: GV1 + BL57 + BL25 [Secondary empirical option: LU6 (Sawada point)].
  - **Chronic Prolapse Stage**: GV20 (moxibustion, 3–7 cones) + GV1 + EX-UE2 + ST36 (tonifying method).
- **Needling Depth & Safety**: GV1 must be inserted **obliquely upward at 40°–60° along the anterior coccyx to 0.8–1.0 cun (18.4–23.0 mm)**. **Perpendicular puncture is strictly contraindicated** due to risk of rectal wall perforation.

## 2. Anatomical & Pathophysiological Targets

1. **Anal Cushion Breakdown**:
   - Anal cushions are specialized vascular complexes located in the left lateral, right anterolateral, and right posterolateral positions.
   - Chronic straining, elevated intra-abdominal pressure, and venous stasis disrupt supportive connective tissues, causing sliding and engorgement of the anorectal venous plexus.
2. **Dual Innervation Across the Pectinate Line**:
   - **Above the Pectinate Line (Internal Hemorrhoids)**: Innervated by autonomic visceral afferents (pelvic splanchnic nerves S2-S4 and sympathetic hypogastric nerves). Lacks somatic sensory receptors; presents primarily with painless, bright-red bleeding and prolapse. Drains via the superior rectal vein into the portal venous system.
   - **Below the Pectinate Line (External Hemorrhoids)**: Innervated by the somatic **inferior rectal nerve (FMA:21865)**. Highly sensitive to pain, temperature, and touch; thrombosis triggers severe acute pain. Drains via inferior and middle rectal veins into the caval system.
3. **Anal Sphincter Spasm Vicious Cycle**:
   - Severe pain and inflammation evoke reflex spasm of the external anal sphincter (innervated by S2-S4 via the pudendal nerve). Elevated anal resting pressure further restricts hemorrhoidal venous return, intensifying tissue ischemia and swelling.

## 3. Classical Literature Analysis & Critique of LU6

### 1) Orthodox Classical Formulations in *Zhengzhi Tongjian*
Full screening of classical hemorrhoid chapters indicates clear therapeutic convergence:
- **Local Point**: **Changqiang (GV1)** — Luo-connecting point of the Governor Vessel. Ancient surgical traditions of bleeding, cauterization, and local decompression.
- **Distal Point**: **Chengshan (BL57)** — Bladder Meridian on the gastrocnemius junction. Universal choice for hemorrhoids across imperial dynasties.
- **Sacral Segmental Points**: **Ciliao (BL32)**, **Yaoshu (GV2)**, **Dachangshu (BL25)**.
- **Systemic Suspension**: **Baihui (GV20)** — Moxibustion for chronic prolapsing hemorrhoids.

### 2) The Origin of Kongzui (LU6) for Hemorrhoids: Ken Sawada's Empirical Innovation vs. Textual Fallacy

In modern clinical acupuncture, it is widely claimed that *"Kongzui (LU6) treats hemorrhoids because it is the Xi-cleft point of the Lung Meridian, arresting acute bleeding, and because of the Lung-Large Intestine interior-exterior relationship."* However, textual criticism demonstrates that **this is not an authentic classical theory** (Cho & Kim, 2021).

```
[Historiographical Evolution of LU6's Hemorrhoid Indication]

  [Classical Literature (Pre-Qing Dynasty)]
  *Neijing*, *Jiayijing*, *Qianjin Yaofang*, *Zishengjing*, *Dacheng*, etc.
  → Zero records of hemorrhoid treatment (only respiratory and elbow pain indications).
                     │
                     ▼
  [1930s Japan: Ken Sawada (澤田健)]
  *Shinkyu Shinzui* (1941, by Bunshi Shirota)
  → First designated as Sawada's "original point" for hemorrhoids and diagnostic tender point.
    (Located 3 finger-breadths below Chize LU5, distinct from WHO standard).
                     │
                     ▼
  [1950s–1960s Modern Textbooks: Ex-Post-Facto Rationalization]
  1958 *Zhenjiuxue Jiangyi*, 1963 *Shiyong Zhongguo Zhenjiu Jingxuexue*, etc.
  → Sawada's empirical point was integrated and retroactively rationalized
    using classical "Xi-cleft point" and "Lung-Large Intestine" analogies.
```

1. **Total Absence in Pre-Modern Classics**:
   - Comprehensive surveys of ancient and medieval Chinese and Korean medical canons fail to identify a single instance of LU6 indicated for hemorrhoids or hematochezia.
2. **True Provenance: Ken Sawada (1877–1938)**:
   - Ken Sawada introduced this empirical point in Tokyo, documented by his student Bunshi Shirota in *Shinkyu Shinzui* (1941). Sawada located it at "three finger-breadths below LU5," notably different from the WHO standard location (7 cun above the wrist crease).
3. **Rejection of Theoretical Confabulation**:
   - Attributing LU6's hemorrhoid efficacy to "classical Xi-cleft theory" is an ex-post-facto theoretical overlay. Clinicians should recognize that orthodox classical treatment rests upon GV1, BL57, and BL32, while LU6 should be appreciated strictly as a modern empirical adjunct.

## 4. Neuroanatomical & Reflex Mechanisms

1. **Sacral Segmental Regulation (GV1)**:
   - Acupoint GV1 lies over the anococcygeal body (FMA:20273) and levator ani (FMA:19087). Needle stimulation directly activates the inferior rectal nerve (FMA:21865), interrupting the spasm-ischemia-pain cycle.
2. **Distal Somato-visceral Convergence (BL57)**:
   - BL57 over the gastrocnemius (FMA:22541) and soleus (FMA:22542) transmits sensory input through the tibial nerve (FMA:19035) into the S1-S2 dorsal horn.
   - Spinal convergence at S2 allows distal limb stimulation to modulate autonomic and somatic outflow to the pelvic floor, reducing anal hypertonia without local invasive risk.
3. **Ascending Reticular & Motor Suspension (GV20)**:
   - Thermal moxibustion at the vertex stimulates trigeminal and upper cervical afferents (C2), enhancing tonic postural motor drive to the pelvic diaphragm via the brainstem reticulospinal tract.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

## 7. Safety and Clinical Management

1. **Strict Asepsis**:
   - Double disinfection with povidone-iodine or chlorhexidine is mandatory before needle insertion at GV1 to prevent perianal infection.
2. **Surgical Triage**:
   - Massive active hemorrhage causing hemodynamic instability, gangrenous strangulated hemorrhoids, or perianal abscess requires immediate emergency surgical referral.
3. **Herbal Fumigation and Sitz Baths**:
   - Complementing acupuncture with warm decoctions of Sophora flower (*Flos Sophorae*), Sophora root (*Radix Sophorae Flavescentis*), and Phellodendron bark (*Cortex Phellodendri*) markedly reduces mucosal edema and optimizes clinical recovery.
