## 🎯 Comprehensive Verification Conclusion and Academic Findings

> **Core hypothesis test**: 
> **"When the same alias appears among the names of different acupoints, the indications of the two different points may be mixed, or they may be operated together in upper-lower, near-distant point combinations (配穴)." ➔ [v2.7 verdict: partially supported — alias sharing confirmed in the classics for 14/24 pairs (58%); causation of indication mixing and integration through point combination not quantitatively verified]**
>
> It is the author's interpretation that, from a complete analysis of the 2,503-point database and the 24 key Google-Sheet pairs, the appearance of the same alias (別名) is not a mere coincidence of naming but can be classified into three mechanisms: **① alignment of functional and organ (臟腑) efficacy**, **② anatomically symmetrical structure and alignment of local indications**, and **③ abbreviation during the copying of texts and textual transmission of the lower He-Sea points (下合穴)** (no classification criteria or quantitative basis given). In particular, the Type 1 explanation that points "share an alias because of aligned efficacy" and the name glosses in each table (e.g. "the ideological origin of 'precious gateway' (金門)") are speculative etymological interpretations excluded under Rank 4, and are therefore left as **the author's judgement**.

## 🔬 Classification of Three Mechanisms Among Points Sharing the Same Alias

```
[Type 1: functional/pathological alignment]  aligned organ (臟腑) efficacy and regulation of Spirit/Original Qi/kidney Yang (e.g. Shimen-Mingmen, Cuanzhu-Guangming)
        ↓
[Type 2: anatomical/muscle-meridian alignment] symmetrical joint/muscle structures and overlapping categories of local indications (not quantitatively verified) (e.g. Yin Shaohai-Yang Shaohai, Futu-Waiqiu)
        ↓
[Type 3: textual transmission & lower He-Sea compatibility] abbreviation in ancient manuscript copying and textual transmission of the large/small-intestine lower He-Sea points (e.g. Shangjuxu-Shanglian, Xiajuxu-Xialian)
```

### 1. Type 1: Functional / Pathological Alignment (Functional/Pathological Alignment)
- **Characteristics**: The points are in completely different locations (e.g. abdomen vs. back, head vs. leg), but they share the same alias because their mechanisms for regulating particular organ efficacies of the body (kidney Yang (腎陽), Original Qi (元氣), calming the Spirit (安神), brightening the eyes (明目)) coincide.
- **Representative cases**: 
  - **CV05 Shimen (石門) vs GV04 Mingmen (命門)** (shared alternative name: `命門`): Shimen, the Front-Mu point of the Sanjiao (三焦募穴) on the abdomen, and Mingmen, a Governor Vessel (督脈) point on the back, both warm kidney Yang (腎陽) and Original Qi (元氣) and treat seminal emission, infertility and low-back pain.
  - **BL02 Cuanzhu (攢竹) vs GB37 Guangming (光明)** (shared alternative name: `光明`): due to the common efficacy of brightening the eyes (明目) and treating eye diseases.

### 2. Type 2: Anatomical / Muscle-Meridian Alignment (Anatomical/Kinetic Alignment)
- **Characteristics**: Located at anatomically symmetrical left-right, upper-lower or medial-lateral joint and muscle regions of the body, their categories of indication overlap (anatomical-substrate hypothesis, ARI not scored; not quantitatively verified). However, none of the three representative pairs of this type is confirmed as sharing an alias in the classics (#11 ✗, #19 △, #9 ✗).
- **Representative cases**:
  - **HT03 Yin Shaohai (少海) vs SI08 Yang Shaohai (小海)** (shared alternative name: `少海`): a symmetrical structure on the medial and lateral sides of the elbow joint; overlapping indication categories of elbow arthralgia, tennis elbow and upper-limb paralysis (not quantitatively verified). ※ A shared alias of 少海 and 小海 is not confirmed in the classics (similar readings).
  - **ST32 Futu (伏兔) vs GB36 Waiqiu (外丘)** (shared alternative name: `外丘`): lateral muscle regions of the thigh and calf; overlapping indication categories of lower-limb paralysis and arthralgia (not quantitatively verified).
  - **GB02 Tinghui (聽會) vs GB03 Shangguan (上關)** (shared alternative name: `上關`): adjacent points in front of / above the ear; overlapping indication categories of tinnitus, deafness and temporomandibular disorders (not quantitatively verified). ※ '上關' is not among the aliases of 聽會 (not confirmed in the classics).

### 3. Type 3: Textual Transmission and Lower He-Sea Compatibility (Textual Transmission & Lower Sea Compatibility)
- **Characteristics**: Forms in which textual indications were transferred because a two-character prefix was dropped in the copying of ancient manuscripts (內經 · 甲乙經) (e.g. `巨虚上廉` ➔ `上廉`), or because the points were linked along the upper-lower, near-distant combination line of the Yangming meridians (陽明經).
- **Representative case**:
  - **ST37 Shangjuxu (上巨虛) vs LI09 Shanglian (上廉)** (shared alternative name: `上廉`): the lower He-Sea point of the large intestine on the Stomach Meridian of Foot Yangming (足陽明胃經) (Juxu Shanglian (巨虛上廉)) and Shanglian of the Large Intestine Meridian of Hand Yangming (手陽明大腸經) bear the same name '上廉' (上巨虛 alias '上廉', 甲乙經 9 ff.). Compatibility and combination of large-intestine-related indications are not quantitatively verified, and in the data of the large-intestine disorders report only 18 of the 36 'Shanglian' rows (50%) explicitly name ST37. 《針灸腧穴通考》 points out the error by which the Ming-dynasty 《針灸聚英》 mixed the indications of ST37 into LI9 (combination and contaminated indications are not regarded as single-point efficacy, Rank 4).

## 🏛️ Historical Changes in Indication and Point-Combination Patterns by Era

1. **Pre-Qin to Wei, Jin and Northern-Southern Dynasties (先秦·魏晉南北朝)**:
   - Distal point selection along the meridian course (循經), centred on the affected meridian (本經).
   - The notation abbreviating 'Juxu Shanglian (巨虛上廉)' to 'Shanglian (上廉)' in ancient texts (《針灸甲乙經》 9 ff.) caused the name to overlap with Shanglian of the Hand Yangming (the causal claim that this was the "origin" of the mixing is the author's judgement).
2. **Sui-Tang to Song-Jin-Yuan (隋唐·宋金元)**:
   - Marked expansion of moxibustion (灸法) on the abdomen and lower limbs (Guanyuan (關元), Shenque (神闕), Qihai (氣海), Sanyinjiao (三陰交)).
   - For the establishment of upper-lower near-distant point combinations (e.g. `Shanglian + Shangjuxu`, `Yin Shaohai + Yang Shaohai`), the corresponding prescriptions were not found in the three classics (author's judgement, not quantitatively verified).
3. **Ming-Qing period (明清時代)**:
   - Systematic combination with spleen-stomach organ pattern identification and establishment of Back-Shu/Front-Mu pairing (俞募配穴) (Dachangshu (大腸俞) + Tianshu (天樞)).
   - [v2.7 correction] The **手上廉/足上廉** distinction began in Volume 191 of the Song-dynasty 《聖濟總錄》 but was not adopted by later texts, and the Ming-dynasty 《針灸聚英》 mixed the indications of ST37 into LI9 (《針灸腧穴通考》). The claim that the distinction was "completed" in the Ming-Qing period does not match the classics.
4. **Modern and contemporary period (近代·現代)**:
   - Mapping to Western disease names (bacillary dysentery, acute and chronic colitis, tennis elbow, upper-limb paralysis after stroke) completed.
   - Settled by the WHO international standard names and locations (B1-05). "Standardisation of point-combination guidelines" is the author's judgement (no guideline literature cited).
