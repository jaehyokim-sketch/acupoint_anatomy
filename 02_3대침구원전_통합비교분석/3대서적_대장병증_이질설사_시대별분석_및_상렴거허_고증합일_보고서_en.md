# 🏛️ Large-Intestine Disorders and Dysentery/Diarrhoea Across the Eras in the Three Classics, and the Textual Proof That 'Shanglian' Meant 'Shangjuxu': An Integrated Comparative Report

> **Framework**: v2.7 (refined 2026-10-04) · **Scope**: Rank 1–4 textual verification (Rank 5 where needed) · **ARI**: not scored (textual-verification report) · **Source data**: `3대서적_대장병증_이질_설사_침구치료_시대별_경혈분석.csv/.xlsx` (538 keyword-hit snippet rows), `3대서적_대장병증_이질설사_시대별분석_및_상렴거허_고증합일_보고서.csv/.xlsx`
> **Note on script**: Classical quotations and TCM terms are given in Traditional Chinese (繁體字). The cited source files and data are simplified-script editions, so file names and search keywords in `code` are given exactly as they appear on disk and in the data.

This integrated report compares and fuses two studies built on the database (45 PDFs, some 4,200 pages) of the three specialist acupuncture classics *Zhongguo Zhenjiu Xuewei Tongjian* (《中國針灸穴位通鑑》), *Zhongguo Zhenjiu Zhengzhi Tongjian* (《中國針灸證治通鑑》) and *Zhenjiu Shuxue Tongkao* (《針灸腧穴通考》): **① a quantitative, era-by-era analysis of the acupoints used in acupuncture-moxibustion treatment of large-intestine disorders and dysentery/diarrhoea (Report A)**, and **② an evidence-based textual verification that 'Shanglian' (上廉) and 'Xialian' (下廉) in the ancient literature were abbreviations of 'Shangjuxu' (上巨虛) and 'Xiajuxu' (下巨虛) (Report B)**.

> ⚠️ **v2.7 reading caution**: The "frequency of use" in Report A is not a count of prescriptions or treatment records; it is the **number of snippet rows** in which a keyword (`大肠` · `泄泻` · `肠炎` · `痢疾` · `腹泻` · `下利`) was hit on a page of the three-classics PDFs. Of the 182 rows for the Pre-Qin to Wei-Jin period, 157 (86%) are hits on `大肠` (many of them the meridian name '手陽明大腸經'), and only `痢疾` 2 rows, `泄泻` 9 rows, `腹泻` 4 rows and `下利` 2 rows are dysentery/diarrhoea keywords. The same page was counted as several rows (Shanglian 36 rows = 21 unique pages), and the rule used to assign eras is not recorded in the data.

## 🎯 Integrated Comparison Summary: Quantitative Statistics (Report A) vs. Textual Verification (Report B)

> **Integrated conclusion (v2.7 refinement)**: 
> Among the snippets classified under the Pre-Qin to Wei-Jin period in **Report A**, **'Shanglian (上廉)' ranked 2nd (36 rows)**. On re-reading the original snippets, **18 of the 36 rows (50%) explicitly name `巨虚上廉`/`上巨虚` (ST37)**, **14 rows (39%) are point entries for Shanglian of the Hand Yangming (上廉, LI9) or pages on the 手陽明經**, and 4 rows (11%) cannot be determined (Xialian 25 rows: 5 explicitly ST39, 16 related to LI8 / the Hand Yangming, 4 indeterminate). Report B's finding that "the ancient record 'Shanglian' was an abbreviation of Shangjuxu (ST37)" is therefore **supported by the half of the snippets that explicitly name ST37**, but the conclusion that all 36 rows refer to ST37 is not supported by the data. *Zhenjiu Shuxue Tongkao* (《針灸腧穴通考》) itself, discussing "上廉" in a prescription in Volume 13 of 《醫心方》, admits an undecidable case: "難以判斷此方中'上廉'是手上廉，還是足上廉".

## 🔬 Key Discoveries from the Integrated Three-Classics Perspective

### 1. Why was 'Shanglian (上廉)' the second-ranked dysentery/diarrhoea point in the ancient literature?
- **Quantitative finding (Report A)**: In the snippets classified as Pre-Qin to Wei-Jin, 'Shanglian' was hit in 36 rows (21 unique pages), ranking next after Dachangshu (52 rows). However, because 86% of the hit keywords are `大肠`, it should be read as "2nd in hits for large-intestine-related keywords", not "2nd for dysentery/diarrhoea".
- **Textual cause (Report B)**: As shown by *"大腸合入於巨虛上廉"* in the Benshu (本輸) chapter of the *Huangdi Neijing · Lingshu* (《黃帝內經·靈樞》), the original name was **'Juxu Shanglian (巨虛上廉)'**, and from Volume 9 of 《針灸甲乙經》 onward a number of texts abbreviated it to **'上廉'** (*Zhongguo Zhenjiu Xuewei Tongjian* (《中國針灸穴位通鑑》), Shangjuxu (上巨虛) alias entry: "上廉。"《針灸甲乙經》9:17 ff.). This is, however, **an abbreviation in the original notation**; no basis was found in the classics for asserting that it was "an error in which two characters were dropped during copying" (author's judgement). Moreover, the same '上廉' is used in 《靈樞·經脈》 as a regional term, as in "循臂上廉", so the name overlaps with Shanglian of the Hand Yangming (LI9) (a target for Rank 3 homonym separation).

### 2. Historical differentiation of Shanglian of the Hand Yangming (LI9) and Shangjuxu of the Foot Yangming (ST37), and their integration in point combinations
- **When the names were distinguished (corrected)**: According to the name-and-substance study of Shanglian (LI9) in *Zhenjiu Shuxue Tongkao* (《針灸腧穴通考》), the Song-dynasty 《聖濟總錄》 changed it to "手上廉" to distinguish it from the Foot Yangming "上廉", but later texts did not follow this and continued to use '上廉', and the Ming-dynasty 《針灸聚英》 actually mixed the indications of ST37 into LI9. The earlier text's statement that "the distinction was completed in the Ming-dynasty 《針灸聚英》 and 《針灸大成》" runs in the opposite direction to the classics and is corrected.
- **Clinical near-distant point combination (配穴)**: Because no prescription with the combination 'Shanglian (LI9) + Shangjuxu (ST37) + Tianshu' was found in the three classics, the statement that it was "established" is left as **author's judgement, not quantitatively verified**. Further, under the Rank 4 principle (exclusion of transfer of combination indications), indications derived from combination prescriptions are not transcribed as single-point efficacy ($P_{single}$) of LI9 or ST37.

### 3. Limitations (added in v2.7)
- The era classification is not the era of the text cited by the original snippet but a label attached by the extraction script, and its rule is not preserved in the data (e.g. rows labelled Pre-Qin to Wei-Jin include pages citing modern texts such as 《針灸穴名解》 and 《針灸學簡編》).
- The keyword `大肠` also hits meridian names (手陽明大腸經) and organ names, so hits cannot be equated with "records of treating large-intestine disorders".
- Conclusions about the anatomical substrate are outside the scope of this report (Ranks 6 and 7 not performed).
