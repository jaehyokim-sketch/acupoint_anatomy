Historical Collation Summary (Cho Eunbyul, Kim Jae-Hyo et al. 2021): In ancient East Asian medical literature, treating haemorrhoids with Kongzui was completely non-existent. In 1941, Sawada Ken (澤田健) created a unique diagnostic and therapeutic reaction point 3 finger-breadths below Chize (LU5) in Shinkyushinzui (《針灸真髓》). This Japanese clinical experience was transmitted through Taiwan (Guo Jialiang 1963) to Korea (Lee Suho 1971) and became codified as an official textbook indication in Jeonghae Chimguhak (1974) and all subsequent standard textbooks. During this transmission, the specific location (3 finger-breadths below Chize) and its primary role as a tactile diagnostic point were omitted, and the indication was grafted onto the 12-cun standard Kongzui (7 cun above the wrist) alongside post-hoc rationales of "Lung-Large Intestine interior-exterior relation" and "Xi-cleft point theory." This constitutes a classic textual mistransmission (訛傳).

  • The T2 site (approx. 3 finger-breadths / 2–3 cun below Chize 尺澤) lies over the proximal junction between brachioradialis and pronator teres. In the subcutaneous layer passes the lateral cutaneous nerve of the forearm, and deep to it run the bifurcation of the radial nerve trunk into superficial and deep branches and the radial recurrent artery (inferred from B1-01 and B1-03). In Sawada's tradition and Japanese classical acupuncture, rather than relying on an abstract geometric measurement, acupuncturists locate this point by manually palpating for tactile pathological reaction points (tenderness, induration, and subcutaneous depression/fullness) (Cho et al. 2021).

7. Spatial Discrepancy Branch Inference Diagram

  • Reason for the split & spatial discrepancy: The distance between T1 (7 cun above the wrist / 5 cun below Chize) and T2 (approx. 3 finger-breadths / 2–3 cun below Chize) is about 2–3 cun (approx. 42–62 mm), markedly exceeding the 15 mm discrepancy threshold ($\Delta_{discrepancy} \gg 15\text{ mm}$).
  • The essence of Sawada Ken's point location (Cho Eunbyul, Kim Jae-Hyo et al. 2021): 1. The emergence of Sawada's Kongzui (T2) was not merely a computational artifact of the early-modern Japanese 10-cun forearm scale. Sawada Ken explicitly recognized the classical standard location (12-cun scale, 7 cun above wrist / 5 cun below Chize), but asserted that "locating it at the ordinary location (5 cun below Chize) is ineffective for haemorrhoids (如依普通取法則不見效)." He argued that one must locate it significantly closer to Chize, at "approximately three finger-breadths below Chize (大抵尺澤の下三橫指位の処, approx. 2–3 cun)" (his unique point). 2. Crucially, in Sawada's practice and Japanese classical acupuncture, T2 functioned not merely as a treatment point, but primarily as a "diagnostic reaction point for haemorrhoids and anal disorders (魄門 診斷點)":
    • Shinkyūshinzui (1941): "When haemorrhoids are present, a distinct tactile sensation or tenderness is felt at Kongzui (痔瘡在孔最有觸覺)."
    • Gyeonghyeolhaeseol (2007): "If there is a marked left-right difference in tenderness at Kongzui, one can predict that there is definitely a problem in the anal/buttock region. Through the interior-exterior relationship of Lung and Large Intestine, buttock congestion manifests along the Lung meridian, and Kongzui is used to treat this congestion."
    • Shinkyū Jitsuyō Keiraku (1972): "As the Xi-cleft point of the Lung, this site exhibits marked meridian fluctuations such as tenderness or subcutaneous depression/fullness (陷下膨隆)... Because Xi-cleft points gather deep qi and blood, it relieves haemorrhoid congestion and bleeding." 3. Thus, T2 is not an abstract geometric point, but an "A-shi (阿是) reactive locus palpated for tenderness, induration, and tissue depression/fullness approx. 3 finger-breadths below Chize."
  • Branch decision: $\Delta\text{ARI-Core} = 71.2 - 35 = \mathbf{36.2 \ge 10}$, and confidence intervals $[71.2, 71.2]$ and $[35, 35]$ do not overlap $\rightarrow$ T1 declared superior. The canonical anatomical substrate of Kongzui is established at T1 (7 cun above the wrist), while "Kongzui treats haemorrhoids" is separated as an empirical claim regarding T2 (the 3-finger-breadth reaction point).

③ Classical Acupoint Selection for Haemorrhoids & Evidence Pyramid Evaluation (Basis: Cho et al. 2021)

  1. Analysis of Classical Literature (pre-1900) for Haemorrhoids (Table 3): - A complete survey of 13 major classical treatises across China, Korea, and Japan (Jiayi Jing, Qianjin Yaofang, Waitai Miyao, Yixinfang, Bianque Xinshu, Zhenjiu Zisheng Jing, Zhenjing Zhinan, Hyangyak Jipseongbang, Chijong Jinam, Zhenjiu Dacheng, Donguibogam, Chimgu Gyeongheombang, Yizong Jinjian):
    • More than 90% of all acupoints selected for haemorrhoids belong to local perineal/perianal points (Huiyin CV1, Changqiang GV1, Huiyang BL35, local A-shi collateral bloodletting) and distal lower-limb points of the Bladder Meridian (Chengshan BL57, Chengjin BL56, Weizhong BL40, Feiyang BL58, Chengfu BL36), Kidney Meridian (Fuliu KI7), and Spleen Meridian (Shangqiu SP5). This perfectly aligns with classical meridian symptomatology (suoshengbing of the Bladder meridian includes "痔", and pathology of the Governor Vessel includes "痔疾").
    • Upper extremity points were exceptionally rare: Laogong (PC8) and Yanggu (SI5) in Qianjin and Zisheng Jing, Lieque (LU7) in Zhenjing Zhinan, and Erbai (EX-UE2) in Zhenjiu Dacheng.
    • The only Lung Meridian point appearing in pre-1900 classics was Lieque (LU7) paired with Zhaohai (KI6) in Zhenjing Zhinan (1295), which was chosen strictly as one of Dou Hanqing's (竇漢卿) Eight Confluent Points (交經八穴) connecting to the Conception Vessel (任脈), having no connection to Lung-Large Intestine interior-exterior relation.
    • Kongzui (LU6) was completely absent (0 occurrences) from all classical acupuncture formulas for haemorrhoids published before 1900.
  2. Modern Textbooks and Kongzui Inclusion: - China: Pre-2000 modern texts relied on classical Bladder/local points and Erbai (EX-UE2). Kongzui first appeared as a haemorrhoid selection in Xu's Acupuncture and Moxibustion (2002). A survey of 30 modern Chinese textbooks revealed that while 63% include Kongzui, it is strictly relegated to a secondary/modifying point for haematochezia (便後出血), never as a primary point (primary points being BL57, BL54, BL56, BL35, EX-UE2, GV2, GV6, BL58, CV1 at 67–100%), and recent textbooks (2012, 2017) diverge on its inclusion. - Korea: Absent in Jinryo Yogam (1974), Kongzui was introduced in Chimgu Chiryohak (1983) and Chimguhak (1994). The 2017 KCD Clinical Guide lists Kongzui only for moxibustion (灸法), not manual acupuncture.
  3. Evidence Pyramid & Bibliographic Analysis: - Shinkyūshinzui (1941) represents case reports/anecdotal clinical records, the lowest tier of the evidence hierarchy. - Modern research: Limited to small case series (2 out of 3 Korean case studies; 76 cases in China 2018 with ambiguous point photos and no control group). A recent 2020 anal disease clinical study excluded Kongzui entirely. - A 60-year review (1956–2016) of Chinese acupuncture studies on haemorrhoids (74 studies) found frequent upper limb points were Erbai, Chichangxue, Hegu (LI4), Quchi (LI11), and Ximen (PC4), while Kongzui (LU6) was completely excluded. - Comprehensive searches in PubMed, OSF, and Research Registry revealed 0 clinical trials or RCTs for LU6 on haemorrhoids; the only relevant paper in PubMed out of 49 LU6 citations was Huang Longxiang's paper refuting the mistransmission. - Conclusion: The textbook indication of Kongzui for haemorrhoids lacks classical textual grounding and remains at the bottom of the modern evidence pyramid (uncontrolled case reports), representing an unsubstantiated reflex hypothesis.

The anatomical substrate is the subcutaneous and fascial layer at the medial border of brachioradialis (lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm, cephalic vein), and a perpendicular insertion of 23 mm reaches the radial artery and veins and the superficial branch of the radial nerve (Layer 4). The location rests on Huangdi Mingtang Jing (《黃帝明堂經》) "在腕上七寸", but because forearm bone-proportional scales differed between periods (12.5 cun in ancient classics vs 10 cun in early-modern transition vs 12 cun in WHO standard), the actual location relative to the wrist crease varies between 140 and 175 mm, with the contemporary international consensus established at 7 cun above the wrist crease on a 12-cun scale (approx. 146 mm). The reflex mechanism (airway smooth muscle relaxation) is limited to rat studies with point combinations and is not concordant on the B-Check (7/12), so it is described as a hypothesis. With equal weights it falls to C (68.8), so grade B depends on the provisional weights.
  • T2 Sawada Kongzui (尺澤下 3橫指, approx. 2–3 cun) — Undetermined, hypothesis (D·H). This is an early-modern Japanese empirical point and diagnostic reaction locus created by Sawada Ken (1941). The "Kongzui treats haemorrhoids" entry in modern textbooks is a historical mistransmission (訛傳) in which the indication was grafted onto the 7-cun standard point while stripping away its true location (3 finger-breadths below Chize) and diagnostic palpation role (Cho et al. 2021; Tongkao, 附: 孔最治痔溯源; papers.id 1561, 1563, 1723). Spinal segmental concordance is completely lacking (C5–C7 vs S2–S4), and modern evidence remains at the bottom tier of the evidence pyramid (uncontrolled case reports).
  • Branch: $\Delta\text{ARI-Core} = 36.2 \ge 10$, intervals do not overlap $\rightarrow$ T1 superior. The intrinsic indications of Kongzui belong to diaphoresis (熱病汗不出), headache (厥頭痛), cough with counterflow (咳逆), and haemoptysis (吐血/咯血); haemorrhoids (痔疾) are excluded from the canonical T1 indications.

10-6. Clinical Location Guidelines & Recommendations for Textbook Annotation (Cho et al. 2021)

  1. Clinical Location & Palpation Principle (Differentiated Palpation & Location Principle): - When utilizing Kongzui in clinical practice to treat haemorrhoids and relieve perianal congestion, mechanical needling at the WHO standard point (7 cun above the wrist / 5 cun below Chize) is unlikely to yield significant efficacy (concurring with Sawada Ken's clinical observation that "ordinary point location is ineffective for haemorrhoids"). - Following Sawada Ken's original methodology and Japanese classical acupuncture practice, clinicians should locate the point significantly closer to Chize (LU5), approximately three finger-breadths below the cubital crease (approx. 2–3 cun), by carefully palpating for tactile pathological reaction points (tenderness, induration, and subcutaneous depression/fullness).
  2. Dual Role as an Anal Diagnostic Reaction Point (Po-Gate Diagnostic Point 魄門 診斷點): - Kongzui should be utilized not merely as a treatment point, but primarily as a diagnostic reaction point, comparing bilateral tenderness to evaluate and predict the degree of pelvic/haemorrhoidal venous congestion (Gyeonghyeolhaeseol 2007).
  3. Recommendation for Korean Medicine Textbook Committees: - Contemporary Korean textbooks (Gyeonghyeolhak and Acupuncture Medicine) list haemorrhoids (痔, 痔瘡出血) as an official primary indication of Kongzui, yet this has zero classical grounding and extremely fragile modern empirical evidence (uncontrolled case series). - Therefore, future textbook revisions should append an explicit Annotation (註) to provide accurate academic and clinical information: > "① The indication of Kongzui for haemorrhoids does not exist in any ancient Chinese, Korean, or Japanese medical classics, but originated from the early-modern Japanese clinical experience of Sawada Ken (澤田健) published in Shinkyūshinzui (1941). ② When applying Kongzui for haemorrhoidal disorders, clinicians should not needle the standard WHO location (7 cun above the wrist), but must palpate for tenderness and tissue reactions closer to Chize (approx. 3 finger-breadths below Chize), which also functions as an anal diagnostic point. ③ The theoretical justifications of Lung-Large Intestine interior-exterior relation and Xi-cleft point were adopted post-hoc to rationalize Sawada's empirical finding, and rigorous evidence from randomized controlled trials (RCTs) is still required."
