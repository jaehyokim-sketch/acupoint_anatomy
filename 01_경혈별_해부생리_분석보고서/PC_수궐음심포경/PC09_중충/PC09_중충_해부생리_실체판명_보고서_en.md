### Summary of Conclusions

Zhongchong (中衝, PC9) lies in **the fingerprint skin and subcutis at the centre of the tip of the distal phalanx of the middle finger (Layer 1)**. The most probable anatomical substrate is **the terminal branches of the proper palmar digital nerve (sensory) arising from the median nerve and the terminal arterial network of the proper palmar digital arteries**. This judgement, however, rests only on agreement between the two textbooks and standard anatomy texts (B1-01 · B1-03); no study has measured PC9 itself in cadavers or by ultrasound. The reflex mechanism explaining the arousal and emergency effects of bloodletting at the Jing-Well point (井穴) scores 6/12 on the B-Check (not concordant) and is therefore described only as a **hypothesis**. The overall verdict is **ARI-Core 70.0 ± 1.2, grade B (probable)**. It sits on the lower boundary of grade B (70; Rater A's 68.75 is C), and the equal-weight sensitivity is 67.7 (C), so the **weight-sensitive** flag is applied. The rise in grade comes from reflecting the transcription-error collation of the *Jingluo Tongjian* (《經絡通鑑》) in $S_{orig}$ ④ (Section 10.2). The needling safety index is **SI 100** (every text gives 1 fen = 2.3 mm, and no text records a contraindication to needling).

**Mixed attribution (Heart/Pericardium)**: (history of commentary added from the *Jingluo Tongjian*: Zhang Jiebin '心與心胞，本同一藏', Zhang Zhicong '中衝，包絡之經也', Ma Shi '不言心經者' — [Jingluo Tongjian 26] L262·266, [Jingluo Tongjian 22] L295) 《靈樞·本輸》 records Zhongchong as '心出於中衝 …… 手少陰也', treating it as the Jing-Well point of the Heart (心, hand Shaoyin (手少陰)) (Tongkao L1642). Attribution to the Pericardium (心包, hand Jueyin (手厥陰)) first appears in the *Huangdi Mingtang Jing* (《黃帝明堂經》): '心主出中衝 …… 手心主脈之所出也' (Tongkao L1643). At the stage of 《靈樞·本輸》 the Pericardium Meridian (手厥陰心包經) did not have its own series of Five Transport points (五輸穴), so the points now on the Pericardium Meridian were listed under the name of the Heart Meridian. Ordering the sources chronologically resolves this mixing. No case of confusion with the names of other finger Jing-Well points such as Shaochong (少衝, HT9) or Guanchong (關衝, TE1) was found in the classics.

**Insertion reference point**: the palpation phrase for Target 2 is '陷者中' (in the depression), but the fingertip has no muscles or tendons and hence no 分肉 · 兩筋間 · 緣 type gap → **gap unclear; no insertion reference point is marked**. The reference structure is the free edge of the nail (nail, FMA:54335).

**Note on the variant location**: estimated from typical distal-phalanx dimensions, the distance between the centre of the tip and the radial nail-root-corner variant is about 10–15 mm (not a point-specific measurement). This distance may exceed the new branching threshold (Δ > 10 mm). However, the variant is a later alternative view arising from 《竇太師針經》 (Yuan) onward under the influence of '中指內廉' in 《靈樞·邪客》 and the location of Laogong (勞宮) (*Tongkao* 名實考), and it corresponds to none of the Tri-Target definitions (Target 1 earliest text, Target 2 palpation phrase, Target 3 WHO) → under the canonical Rank 5 **Target exclusion rule** it is **not set as a Target and is neither branched nor scored**. Zhongchong is scored as a single Target (Section 7). Both positions lie within the terminal distribution of the same proper palmar digital nerves and arteries.

### 10.6 Final Conclusion

The anatomical substrate of Zhongchong (中衝, PC9) is best regarded, at **grade B (probable)** (ARI-Core 70.0 ± 1.2, weight-sensitive — on the B lower boundary), as **the terminals of the proper palmar digital nerve (median nerve, sensory) and the terminal arterial network of the proper palmar digital arteries in the fingerprint skin and subcutis of the tip of the middle finger (Layer 1)**. The location has remained the centre of the tip from 《靈樞·本輸》 and the *Mingtang Jing* (《明堂經》) through to the WHO standard, and the three Tri-Target positions (classical text, palpation-based locus, WHO) all merge within 10 mm (Target 1≡2≡3). The radial nail-corner variant is a later alternative view arising from the influence of the location of Laogong (勞宮) and of '中指內廉' in 《靈樞·邪客》 (*Tongkao* 名實考); the 《邪客》 text and commentaries collected in the *Jingluo Tongjian* also read '內廉' as a meridian path rather than a point location. Needling to 1 fen (2.3 mm) or pricking to bleed is very safe (SI 100). However, there is no PC9-only evidence for the emergency and arousal effects of Jing-Well bloodletting, which are likely to be a non-specific response to noxious stimulation, so the reflex mechanism remains at the hypothesis stage. Raising the verdict would require ultrasound measurement of the fingertip soft tissue at PC9 and human studies comparing PC9-only stimulation with stimulation of other fingertips (controls).
