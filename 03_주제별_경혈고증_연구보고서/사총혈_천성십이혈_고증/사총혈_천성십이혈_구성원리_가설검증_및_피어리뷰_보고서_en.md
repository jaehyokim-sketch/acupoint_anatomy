## 0. Summary of Overall Verdicts

| Domain | Verdict | Key evidence |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Methods** | **C (inadequate)** | The selection criteria and search scope for the texts reviewed are not stated. Although many verses (歌賦) deal with region-specific point selection, only 4–6 were chosen for review. The primary origins of the principle (《靈樞·終始》, 《小品方》) are omitted. There are errors in quotation of the original text |
| **Result 1: disease site → related meridian** | **B (largely valid)** | Three of the four Four Command Points are directly explained by meridian pathways. Lieque (列缺)→head and nape requires Luo-Connecting (絡穴) / interior–exterior (表裏) logic rather than the pathway. Two of the six pairs of the Twelve Heavenly Star Points (Taichong–Kunlun, Tongli–Lieque) pair different meridians and fall outside the "same-meridian pair" explanation |
| **Result 2: distal points below the elbow and knee** | **C+ (counter-example exists)** | **Huantiao (環跳, GB30, hip region)** appears in the Eleven Heavenly Star Points (天星十一穴), the Twelve Heavenly Star Points (天星十二穴) and the Qianjin Eleven Points (千金十一穴) alike, refuting the universality of the principle |
| **Result 3: representative points based on similarity of indications** | **D (not verified)** | No data or analysis supporting this principle is presented. The authors themselves acknowledge that "future experimental verification is needed" |
| **"Four Command Points → Twelve Heavenly Star Points expansion" model** | **D (chronological reversal)** | The Eleven Heavenly Star Points (《玉龍經》, Yuan 1329) predate the Four Command Points literature (Ming 《乾坤生意》, 《針灸大全》 1439). The direction **compression (Heavenly Star → Four Command)** rather than expansion fits the chronology |
| **Conclusion** | **C+ (partial over-generalisation)** | The conclusion's wording "presumed to be the most effective" is cautious. However, it generalises "a common principle of major acupoint composition" from only 4–6 verses, and the claim that this helps clinical point selection has no supporting evidence |

**One-line assessment**: Organising the Four Command Point family of verses within a general point-selection framework of "region-specific, distal, representative points" is educationally useful. It is, however, **at the level of proposing a hypothesis**. Because it lacks comparison with primary sources, chronological alignment, handling of counter-examples and quantitative verification of indication similarity, the strength of the conclusion that it "revealed a common composition principle" exceeds its evidence.

## 3. Primary Source vs. Secondary Quotation and Chronological Alignment (Rank 3)

| Date | Text | Relevant content | Primary / secondary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| Pre-Qin & Han | 《靈樞·終始》 | Above the waist governed by Hand-Taiyin (手太陰) and Yangming (陽明), below the waist by Foot-Taiyin (足太陰) and Yangming (陽明) (distal upper–lower correspondence) | Primary |
| Eastern Jin to Northern & Southern Dynasties | 《小品方》 (citing 《曹氏灸經》) | Diseases of the head → points on the arm; diseases of the heart and abdomen → points on the shin and foot | Primary |
| **Yuan 1329** | 《扁鵲神應針灸玉龍經》 《天星十一穴歌訣》 | 11 points (no Taichong (太衝)) | **Primary (earliest verse on major points)** |
| Early Ming | Zhu Quan (朱權) 《乾坤生意》 | First appearance of the Four Command Points (as stated in the paper) | Primary |
| Ming 1439 | Xu Feng (徐鳳) 《針灸大全》 | 《四總穴歌》, 《千金十一穴歌》, 《馬丹陽天星十二穴並治雜病歌》 (Taichong (太衝) added) | Primary / compilation |
| Ming 1529 | Gao Wu (高武) 《針灸聚英》 | Transmission | Secondary quotation |
| Ming 1575 | Li Chan (李梴) 《醫學入門》 | 360 points → 66 points → summarised in 8 points; "百病一鍼爲率, 多則四鍼" | Reinterpretation |
| Ming 1601 | Yang Jizhou (楊繼洲) 《針灸大成》 | Reprint of the Twelve Heavenly Star Points | Secondary quotation |
| Qing 1742 | 《醫宗金鑑》 | Regions for combining the Eight Confluence Points | Reinterpretation |

**Result of chronological alignment**: The paper describes the Four Command Points as a "simple model" and the Twelve Heavenly Star Points as an "expanded version" of the Four Command Points (p.229). However, the order in extant literature is **Eleven Heavenly Star Points (1329) → Four Command Points (early Ming to 1439)**. It therefore fits the chronology better to regard the Four Command Points as a **compressed / summarised** form of the Heavenly Star family. In Section 3 the paper itself uses compression logic — "Zusanli and Neiting have similar indications, so Zusanli represents them" — which conflicts internally with its explanation in an "expansion" framework.

## 4. Validity Assessment of Each Result

### 4.1 Principle ①: Disease site → points on the related meridian

| Verse | Point (pair) | Region treated | Explained by meridian pathway? | Remarks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| Four Command | Zusanli (足三里) ST36 | 肚腹 | Yes | Foot-Yangming (足陽明) passes through the abdomen |
| Four Command | Weizhong (委中) BL40 | 腰背 | Yes | Foot-Taiyang (足太陽) passes through the back and lumbar region |
| Four Command | Lieque (列缺) LU7 | 頭項 | **No** | The Hand-Taiyin (手太陰) main meridian does not reach the head and nape. Logic of connecting to Hand-Yangming (手陽明) as the Luo-Connecting point (interior–exterior (表裏), Luo vessels (絡脈)) is needed |
| Four Command | Hegu (合谷) LI4 | 面口 | Yes | Hand-Yangming (手陽明) passes through the face and mouth |
| Heavenly Star 12 | Zusanli (足三里)–Neiting (內庭) | Abdomen | Yes (same-meridian pair) | |
| Heavenly Star 12 | Quchi (曲池)–Hegu (合谷) | Head and face | Yes (same-meridian pair) | |
| Heavenly Star 12 | Weizhong (委中)–Chengshan (承山) | Lower back & lower limb | Yes (same-meridian pair) | |
| Heavenly Star 12 | Huantiao (環跳)–Yanglingquan (陽陵泉) | Lateral side | Yes (same-meridian pair) | |
| Heavenly Star 12 | **Taichong (太衝)–Kunlun (崑崙)** | Head & eyes / lower back & lower limb | **Different-meridian pair** (LR+BL) | A pair not explained by the expansion model of attaching one partner to each of the four Four Command Points and adding a lateral pair |
| Heavenly Star 12 | **Tongli (通里)–Lieque (列缺)** | Heart & chest / lung & throat | **Different-meridian pair** (HT+LU) | The Qianjin Eleven Points instead pair "Houxi (後谿)–Lieque (列缺)" |

- **Verdict**: At the level of individual points, Principle ① is largely correct (12 of 13 points can be explained by the pathway of their own meridian).
- However, **the explanation at the "pairing" level fits only 4 of the 6 pairs.**
- The text (p.229) explains with the pairs of the Qianjin Eleven Points (Weizhong–Kunlun, Houxi–Lieque), while Fig.1B explains with the pairs of the Twelve Heavenly Star Points (Weizhong–Chengshan, Kunlun–Taichong, Lieque–Tongli). **In effect, pairs from different verses are mixed.**

### 4.2 Principle ②: Distal points below the elbow and knee

| Point | Location | Below the elbow/knee? |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| ST36, ST44, LI4, LU7, BL57, BL60, HT5, LR3, SI3, GB34 | Forearm & hand, leg & foot | Yes |
| LI11 Quchi (曲池), BL40 Weizhong (委中) | Level of the elbow / knee (He-Sea points (合穴)) | Borderline (may be counted as "below" within the Five Transport Points (五輸穴) system) |
| **GB30 Huantiao (環跳)** | **Gluteal region, hip joint area** | **No** |

- **Verdict**: Huantiao appears in **all three** verses — the Eleven Heavenly Star Points, the Twelve Heavenly Star Points and the Qianjin Eleven Points. Yet the paper presents "selected from distal sites below the elbow and knee" as a common principle **without addressing the Huantiao counter-example.**
- Principle ② should be toned down and described as a tendency ("most (12/13)").
- In addition, the link drawn between the "360 → 66 → 8" summary of the 《醫學入門》 and the Four Command Points is an analogy (類比), not a proven basis for their composition.

### 4.3 Principle ③: Representative points based on similarity of indications

- The paper states that, among the distal points of the Foot-Yangming meridian (足陽明經) (ST36, 37, 39, 41, 43, 44, 45) and the Hand-Yangming meridian (手陽明經) (LI1–5, LI11), "the representative ones" were chosen.
- However, **no criteria, data or analysis for judging representativeness are presented.**
- A cluster study of the similarity of Source-point (原穴) indications (reference 12, Jung 2015) is cited, but it is not an analysis comparing the Four Command / Heavenly Star points with other points on the same meridian.
- The example given as evidence also contains a **code error (Daling (大陵) PC6 → PC7)**.
- **Verdict**: An unverified hypothesis. The authors themselves state that "whether these are the most effective acupoints needs future experimental verification".

## 5. Anatomical and Neural-Segment Verification (Rank 5·6: Layers A·B) — Anatomical Substrate Hypothesis (ARI not calculated)

> **v2.7 label**: The "relation" verdicts (segmental / non-segmental) in this section are **anatomical substrate hypotheses** that have not undergone six-item B-Check scoring or Rank 7 ARI scoring. They serve only as supporting arguments in the evaluation of the paper.

The paper groups "distal point selection" as a single principle. From the neural-segment perspective, the pathways of action of the four Four Command Points divide into **segmental** and **non-segmental (via higher centres)**. In the table below, innervation follows the Kim Jae-hyo dataset and the common-textbook dataset (B2-09), and spinal segments follow normative anatomy texts (dermatomes and myotomes of B1-01 Gray's 42nd ed. and B1-03 Moore 8th ed.).

| ST36 Zusanli (足三里) | Lateral sural cutaneous nerve, deep fibular nerve | L4-S1 | Abdominal viscera (sympathetic T5-L2, vagus nerve) | **Non-segmental** (higher-centre / vagal pathway) | 102 papers |

**Comparison with Layers A and B (biomedical evidence, citing registry IDs)**
- ST36: **B3-17** (Liu S *et al.*, *Nature* 2021;598:641-645, PMID 34646018, [doi:10.1038/s41586-021-04001-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04001-4); title and DOI reconfirmed via PubMed esummary on 2026-10-04, matching master DB papers.id 317). Low-intensity electroacupuncture at ST36 on the mouse hindlimb drove the vagal–adrenal axis via PROKR2 sensory neurons. The same effect did not occur at abdominal ST25 (preceding somatotopic study **B3-16**).
  - **Scope**: mouse electroacupuncture (registry B3-17 scope), point tested ST36 (PROKR2 fibres in the deep fascia of the limb), target a systemic inflammatory phenotype. For the other Four Command Points (BL40, LU7, LI4), registry rules do not allow more than 'analogy (1 point)'.
  - This result provides **partial physiological support** for the paper's Principle ② that "distal points drive pathways different from proximal points".
  - It is, however, an animal experiment, limited to the single point Zusanli and an anti-inflammatory phenotype. Human translation (B4) and the debate on point specificity (B4-05; B4-04 showing small differences in effect compared with sham needling) were not examined.
- Site specificity of somato-autonomic reflexes: **B3-05** (Sato A, Sato Y, Schmidt RF. *Rev Physiol Biochem Pharmacol* 1997;130:1-328, PMID 9019119; title reconfirmed via PubMed esummary on 2026-10-04). **Scope**: mainly anaesthetised rats; dependent on stimulus intensity and site. It is not a study verifying individual Four Command Points, so it is at the level of analogy.
  - ※ The ST36 entry of the master DB records this reference as "*Physiol Rev* 1997, PMID 9234958", but that PMID is a virology paper (reconfirmed via PubMed esummary on 2026-10-04: "Characterization of a processive form of the vaccinia virus DNA polymerase", *Virology* 1997). **The DB needs correction** (the DB was not modified; as of 2026-10-04 one entry still remains in the DB).
- LU7, SI3 and ST44 have five or fewer papers in the inverted index, so evidence is sparse (after v3.0 resynchronisation LU7 6, SI3 5, ST44 4 papers — still few).

**Synthesis**
- Anatomically, the paper's "distal point selection" is a category mixing **segmental grounds (Weizhong, Lieque)** and **non-segmental grounds (Zusanli, Hegu)**.
- Hence the positional criterion "distal" alone **cannot explain the mechanism of action as a single one.**
- To put forward the paper's two principles as a unified principle, this heterogeneity should be mentioned. It is acknowledged that this is a demand beyond the scope of the paper.

## 8. Recommendations

1. **Add the primary origins**: The distal upper–lower correspondence principle of 《靈樞·終始》 and 《小品方》 should be presented as the origin of Principle ② (the commentary (按語) in 《證治通鑑》 already links the two).
2. **Enlarge the sample of verses**: Tabulate region-specific representative-point verses such as 《通玄指要賦》, 《肘後歌》 and 《雜病穴法歌》 together, and quantify how consistently Principles ① and ② appear (e.g. proportion of distal points, proportion of same-meridian points).
3. **Quantitative verification of Principle ③**: Vectorise the indications of the distal points of the same meridian (exhaustive extraction of indications from 《穴位通鑑》 and 《證治通鑑》), perform cluster analysis, and test whether the Four Command / Heavenly Star points lie close to the cluster centre (medoid). This is the same direction the authors proposed in reference 14.
4. **Handle counter-examples**: Huantiao and the different-meridian pairs (Taichong–Kunlun, Tongli–Lieque) should be discussed separately. For example, one possible interpretation is that the Heavenly Star family treats Bi syndrome (痹證) and motor disorders of the lower limb as an independent domain.
5. **Distinguish mechanistic levels**: Describing distal point selection as segmental (Weizhong, Lieque) and non-segmental (Zusanli, Hegu) would make comparison with Western-medicine discussions of "major acupoints" more precise.
