🏛️ 1. Overview of the Core Textual Verification and Statement of the Problem

The Dazhui point (大椎, GV14) and the Taodao point (陶道, GV13) of the Governor Vessel (督脈) carry the following major academic issues in the acupuncture literature through the ages: 1. Duplication and sharing of names (issue raised in the earlier edition — no basis in the classics): The earlier edition held that some ancient texts, such as 《太平聖惠方》 and 《針灸聚英》, record Taodao as "一名大椎" ("also called Dazhui"), producing name interference between the two points; this record is not found in the three classics (see notes below). - (v2.7 collation note) This record could not be confirmed in the three classics. The Taodao (陶道) entry of 《穴位通鑑》 gives "別名 無", and the Taodao entry of 《腧穴通考》 also lists no alternative name. The "一名" that is confirmed in the classics is "百勞一穴，一名大椎" (《竇太師針經》), which concerns the relationship between Bailao (百勞) and Dazhui, not Taodao. The issue is therefore more faithfully read as confusion arising from '大椎' being used both as a bone name (T1) and as a point name (the depression above it) (《腧穴通考》: "切不可將'大椎'穴與'大椎'骨相混"). - (2026-10-24 refinement note) A full search of every chapter of the three classics finds no instance of "一名大椎" attached to Taodao (陶道). The passages of the two books quoted in the Taodao entry of 《穴位通鑑》 — 《太平聖惠方》 "在項大椎節下間，俯而取之陷者中" (99:3185) and 《針灸聚英》 "主痎瘧寒熱，灑淅，煩滿汗不出…" (1:120) — give no alternative name either, and the entry's [別名] reads "無" (《中國針灸穴位通鑑》 督脈(2) L239, L269, L337, L413). The Taodao entry of 《腧穴通考》 cites the same two books for its indications without recording any alternative name (《針灸腧穴通考》 下 督脈穴-1 L1935, L1941). The only "一名大椎" in the classics is "百勞一穴，一名大椎" (《竇太師針經》, same file L2017). 2. Shift of the vertebral segment (C7 vs T1): Historical change in whether ancient anatomy regarded the 7th cervical vertebra (C7) as '崇骨' (Chonggu) or '項骨' (Xianggu) and the 1st thoracic vertebra (T1) as '大椎', or defined C7 itself as '大椎'. 3. The exegetical substrate of '上陷' (upper depression) vs '下間' (lower interval / inter-segment): The anatomical location of the actual interspinous gaps referred to by the passages "大椎上陷中" and "大椎節下間".

The malaria prescription (大椎 + 內關 + 陶道, 1987) is a combination (配伍) and is not read as single-point efficacy.
Zhenjiu Shuxue Tongkao (《針灸腧穴通考》) "黃龍祥 考: 《素問·氣府論》'大椎上節'者, 頸椎與胸椎之交接處也. 古人指第1胸椎爲大椎, 後世漸以第7頸椎爲大椎, 故陶道與大椎之名往往相代." (v2.7 collation note: this sentence cannot be found in the refined classics. The classics contain no statement that Taodao had the alternative name 'Dazhui'. A Ming-dynasty criticism (《素問注證發微》) that "people wrongly take the high bone of the nape (C7) as 大椎" confirms that the reference for the bone name wavered, but there is no evidence that the point location itself moved.

🔍 3. Results of Testing the Two Core Hypotheses

[Hypothesis 1] Transfer of the name between the bone itself (椎骨) and the interspinous depression (穴位) (partially supported — the positional relationship matches the classics; the '一名大椎' transfer has no basis in the classics)

  • Content: In ancient anatomy '大椎' originally meant the 1st thoracic vertebra (T1 vertebra), the largest and most prominent. The space above the T1 bone (between C7 and T1) therefore became '大椎上' (the present Dazhui point), and the space below the T1 bone (between T1 and T2) became '大椎下' (the present Taodao point).
  • Verification: 《明堂經》 "大椎，在第一椎上陷者中" and "陶道，在項大椎節下間" agree with a description that locates points in the gaps above and below the T1 bone (confirmed in two of the three classics, 《腧穴通考》 and 《穴位通鑑》). "大椎上節" is an expression from 《小品方》 (quoted in 《醫心方》), not from 《素問·氣府論》. The premise that Taodao was called "一名大椎" is not confirmed in the classics (《穴位通鑑》 "別名 無"), so the 'abbreviation-transfer' interpretation is left as author's judgement, not quantitatively verified.

[Hypothesis 2] Simple copying/transcription error (auxiliary verdict)

  • Content: The theory that the two points were swapped through scribal errors in editions during the manuscript transmission of 《千金方》 and similar texts.
  • Verification: Rather than a simple scribal error, it is interpreted as confusion arising when later generations wrongly took the reference bone (T1) of the bone name 'Dazhui' to be C7 (criticised in 《素問注證發微》). However, the periodisation "Han dynasty T1 → Tang, Song and Ming dynasties C7" has not been confirmed era by era in the classics and remains at the level of interpretation (author's judgement). No instance of the 《千金方》 edition-error theory was found in the three classics.

🎓 6. Graduate Supervision Notes and Final Conclusion

  1. Recommended clinical location: In modern practice, setting aside the ancient confusion over skeletal names and locating Dazhui (GV14) below the C7 spinous process, identified by rotating the neck, and Taodao (GV13) below the T1 spinous process, agrees with the current standard (6th/7th editions of 《針灸學》, WHO B1-05). The rotation palpation method is recorded in the Japanese 《校訂引經訣》 (cited in 《腧穴通考》). The comparative judgement that it is "the safest and most valid" is the author's judgement and not quantitatively verified.
  2. Resolving the Taodao–Dazhui name confusion: The record that Taodao was called '一名大椎' is not found in the three classics. The confusion arises because the bone name '大椎' (T1) and the point name Dazhui (the depression above it) share the same characters, so modern clinical records and research should distinguish the bone name from the point name and should not give Taodao the alternative name 'Dazhui'.
  3. Needling safety line: For both points, perpendicular or upward-oblique insertion to a depth of 15–25 mm is standard; insertion deeper than 30 mm risks entering the dural sac (Dural sac), so needling should proceed while palpating the resistance of the interspinous ligament. - (v2.7 note) The figures 15–25 mm and 30 mm are the author's judgement and not quantitatively verified (not collated with the imaging depth studies B2-07 · B2-10). The classical standards are 《腧穴通考》 "沿棘突的傾斜角向上斜刺 0.5~1 寸，不宜深刺，以免損傷脊髓" (common to Dazhui and Taodao) and 《明堂經》 for Taodao "刺入五分". Converted with the body-cun (同身寸) standard (1 fen = 2.3 mm), 0.5–1 cun ≈ 11.5–23 mm and 5 fen ≈ 11.5 mm, shallower than the upper limit of 25 mm in the text.
